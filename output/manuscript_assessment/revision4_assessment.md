# Assessment of Revision 4 against the ERL reviews

Reviewed: ERL_Formatted_Revision4_resolved.pdf (26 pages, dated here by review on 2026-10-27), against reviews/ERL_Review_ERL-125149.pdf (R1 below) and reviews/Review-ERL-125149.pdf (R2 below). Locations are the PDF's printed line numbers. This is an assessment, not an edit to the submitted manuscript. New analyses reported in the PDF but absent from the current analysis scripts/outputs were assessed as presented; their implementation has not been independently verified.

## Overall judgment

The revision substantially addresses the scientific concerns and presents a much stronger case for an association between storm timing and reduced Impact-day ED utilization. Keeping RMW as the primary corridor is defensible given its now explicit definition, the wind-radii and widened-corridor sensitivities, and the exposure map and storm inventory. I would not recommend resubmission until the missing COVID sensitivity, internal sample accounting, prediction-support accounting, and incomplete placebo presentation are resolved. Interpretation should emphasize the comparatively stable Impact finding and the less stable Threat/Cleanup findings.

The new main estimates are larger in absolute units than our earlier estimates. S4 provides a plausible, substantive explanation: predictions now include never-suppressed high-volume ZIPs rather than only the intersection of the two components' prediction supports. This is a change in target population, not automatically an error. It does require an exact accounting of included/excluded ZIPs and consistent implementation across the main, daily, bootstrap, placebo, and sensitivity analyses.

## Priority corrections

1. **Include the COVID exclusion sensitivity (R1 major comment 3).** Neither the main text nor supplement mentions COVID or exclusion of 2020–2022. Leaving out one season at a time is not equivalent to omitting all three years. Add the joint exclusion to Table S2 and a short Results statement. It must use the revised prediction population and the same two-way inference as the current main analysis. Earlier COVID outputs in this checkout use the older restricted support and ZIP-only intervals and cannot simply be pasted into this version.

2. **Reconcile the calendar and reference count (lines 106–113, 166–169).** The stated counts are Threat 64,240, Impact 8,030, Cleanup 64,230, and None 6,740,950. They sum to 6,877,450 ZIP-days, or 1,046 ZIPs times 6,575 days. May–November 2005–2022 contains 3,852 days and gives 4,029,192 ZIP-days; with the stated phase counts, None would be 3,892,692. Even all calendar days of 2005–2022 gives 6,574 days, one fewer than implied by the manuscript. The larger count appears consistent with an older full-calendar panel including 2004-12-31, but that explanation must be checked. State the actual estimation calendar, distinguish it from the bootstrap blocks, and make the counts match the fitted data. This could be a reporting error rather than an estimation error.

3. **Complete the prediction-population accounting (lines 150–155 and S4, lines 482–493).** The supplement reports 1,046 exposed ZIPs, 276 never suppressed, 82 always suppressed, and a restricted set of 671. These sum to 1,029, leaving 17 ZIPs unexplained. There are 688 ZIPs with some suppressed and some reported days; only 671 have finite predictions from both earlier model components. Explain the remaining exclusions, including possible singletons or unsupported count predictions, and state the actual prediction ZIP count and retained population share for each threshold. Explain explicitly that always-suppressed ZIPs contribute mu(c)=c and zero phase contrast under the common-mean assumption, so no undefined count prediction is required. State how support and boundary probabilities are handled in every bootstrap draw. Do not claim 'all exposed ZIP codes' unless that is true after these checks.

4. **Show the placebo distributions (R2 comment 9; lines 170–189 and 229–242).** Combined-scale placebo estimates and empirical p-values are a substantial improvement, and the new explicit non-overlap condition addresses the inclusive-window issue in principle. The requested plots of observed estimates against the randomization distributions are still missing. Add a supplementary figure for both falsification schemes and clarify the empirical two-sided p-value formula, including the finite-simulation correction. State whether label permutations are within ZIP × year-month. Replace the strong conclusion that findings are tied to storm timing 'rather than to seasonality or model structure' with a narrower statement that the tested randomizations did not reproduce the observed pattern. These tests do not rule out all residual confounding or prove a mechanism.

5. **Moderate intensity and Cleanup claims (R2 comments 6 and 8; abstract lines 27–31; discussion lines 246–278).** Reporting percentages, baselines and a common ZIP target is responsive to the nested-sample concern. It still does not create independent categories or establish a dose-response to measured local wind. Prefer 'larger estimated changes at higher cumulative storm-intensity thresholds' over 'the dose-response pattern ... strengthens.' A formal phase-by-intensity analysis is optional if the conclusions remain explicitly descriptive. Cleanup is less stable than Impact: the widened corridor intervals include zero at every threshold, the wind-radii >=34 estimate is slightly negative, and the >=64 Cleanup interval includes zero with both alternative window lengths. The abstract and conclusion should foreground the stable Impact decrease and qualify the rebound. State clearly that the common-target analysis standardizes to the same ZIP-day population and weights; clarify whether it only changes prediction averaging or also restricts fitting data.

6. **Finish exposure and calibration reproducibility (R1 comments 1, 2 and 5; R2 comments 3–5).** Lines 95–97 describe only within-storm mean RMW imputation, whereas the existing corridor construction also uses pressure-group and subsequent within-storm fallback. Make the prose match the code actually used. For the new wind-radii sensitivity, name the exact IBTrACS fields, how quadrants are averaged, handling of missing/zero values, which segments are retained, and how dates/intensity classifications are assigned to additional storms. Explain use of 50-kt radii for the >=55-kt storm subset: storm selection and wind-footprint thresholds are distinct. Give the population-density source, year, land-area calculation, tercile cutpoints, and fitting versus standardization procedure. Define the calculation supporting c=3.8: a median of daily implied means is different from a pooled mean across absent ZIP-days, although either may be chosen transparently for a sensitivity parameter.

## Smaller finishing points

- Add one sentence explaining the 55-kt intermediate threshold; it is not a standard hurricane-category boundary.
- Confirm reference 9 still has 'In Review' status before submission.
- The density comparisons are point estimates and should not be described as established differences between density strata without uncertainty or tests.
- Absolute visits per ZIP-day and visits per 100,000 are different estimands under population weighting. Document the calculation underlying the operational per-100,000 translations (lines 258–261), rather than imply that dividing a population-weighted ZIP-level count by a typical population automatically gives a statewide rate.
- Treat the shared Impact date as an analysis anchor. The first corridor intersection does not necessarily locate the most hazardous day for every ZIP; soften that clause in the abstract.
- Show near-zero interval endpoints with enough precision: '+0.0' in Table S2 obscures whether the interval actually includes zero.
- Figure panels and the map remain fairly small in the assembled PDF. Increase axis and legend lettering and provide journal-resolution separate figure files.
- Clean up the run-on sentences at lines 95–100, 121–124, 166–169 and 482–486. In particular, 'Florida residents to facilities in other states was not captured' needs grammatical correction.
- Ensure the code and outputs for the new all-ZIP prediction target, common target, wind-radii, density, alternative windows and new placebo results are included in the promised reproducibility archive. They are not represented by the older restricted-support scripts alone.

## Suggested emphasis for the revision

The most consistent finding is reduced ED utilization on the shared Impact date. Its direction persists under alternative timing, window lengths and exposure corridors and after individual storm/season omissions. Threat increases remain uncertain and sensitive to recent seasons; Cleanup increases are more sensitive to the exposure corridor and averaging window. These distinctions strengthen the credibility of the paper and avoid overpromising what the sensitivity analyses establish.
